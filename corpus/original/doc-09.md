# Policy Note on Shared Workshop Tool Loans

This note defines a loan policy for the fictional Harbor Bench community workshop. Its goal is to enable members to borrow portable tools while keeping a dependable log of location, condition, and responsibility. The policy covers drills, sanders, meters, and hand tools that the workshop has selected for off-site operation. Large fixed machines remain inside the building.

A member may begin a loan at the front desk during staffed hours. The desk volunteer checks the membership number, confirms the item code, and records the planned return date. A readable receipt repeats those details for the borrower. The desk process should take no more than two minutes, while retaining enough information to locate a missing item quickly.

The standard loan lasts three days. A member may request one extension before the due time if nobody else has reserved the tool. The extension does not require a new visit. The member can contact the synthetic address loans@harborbench.example or inform the desk by phone. The volunteer must display the altered return date in the register and provide explicit confirmation.

Before each loan, the borrower and volunteer perform a meticulous condition inspection. They verify the power cable, guards, battery, case, and visible damage. A short note and photograph record any existing issue. If the item's safety remains uncertain, the item does not leave the workshop. A temporary hold is safer than trying to interpret a defect at the door.

Borrowers must retain the supplied guard and case, protect the tool from moisture, and follow its printed instructions. They may not alter, repair, or detach an identification label. If a tool stops working, the borrower should complete the task by other means and return the item. The main aim is to prevent a minor fault from becoming a serious safety hazard.

Late returns receive a reminder after twenty-four hours. The workshop may suspend new loans after seven days, but a volunteer must confirm whether an agreed extension exists first. A suspension ends when the tool returns or when the coordinator selects another resolution. The policy does not impose automatic fines. A member who loses an item receives a replacement-cost estimate and a chance to explain the circumstances.

Damage reports should separate normal wear from misuse. A dull sanding pad is a predictable consequence of ordinary work. A removed guard is a separate safety matter that requires review. The coordinator should choose a remedy after examining the condition record, listening to the member, and checking the instructions. Equivalent cases should lead to consistent decisions when the evidence matches.

The register contains membership number, item code, timestamps, condition notes, and loan status. It should not include project descriptions or unrelated personal details. Access belongs to trained desk volunteers and the coordinator. Records older than twelve months should be deleted unless an unresolved loss requires retention. A monthly review should find overdue entries and flag gaps in the sequence.

The policy will start as an eight-week pilot. Success means at least 90% of items return on time, every loan has a complete condition record, and no unresolved safety incident occurs. These thresholds are a minimum operational test, not proof that the policy will work for a bigger workshop. The pilot findings should reveal which provision needs improvement before permanent adoption.

Harbor Bench may change this policy after member review. Any revision should have a stated rationale, an effective date, and a contact for questions. The public copy at https://harborbench.invalid/tool-loans should stay synchronized with the desk copy. A short tag, #HarborToolLoan, may support internal filing. The primary purpose remains explicit: render each loan visible, reversible where possible, and resolvable with minimal effort.
