# Questions About a Shared Equipment Calendar

This FAQ explains an invented calendar for a fictional community workshop called Copper Yard. All people, addresses, and organizations are fictional. The main goal is to enable members to choose a slot, use a machine safely, and complete work without blocking the next person.

What does the calendar cover?

It covers tools that one member can reserve at a time. The laser cutter, photo table, and kiln each have a distinct schedule. Hand tools stay outside the system because a booking would add work without solving a clear problem. The calendar can change as the workshop learns which equipment needs a shared slot.

How do I start a booking?

Open the calendar, select a machine, and choose an available block. Add a short project label and the time you require. The form will show setup and cleanup as separate parts of the slot. A simple label such as “blue tile test” is enough. Do not include customer names or private project details.

Why is cleanup included in my slot?

Cleanup is a common underlying cause. Delays follow when a member books only cutting time, because the next member inherits dust, scraps, or a warm surface. Keeping cleanup inside the scheduled slot makes the handoff plain. It also helps staff identify whether the planned block was too short.

Can I alter a booking?

Yes. You can change the scheduled time or reduce the duration until two hours before the slot. After that point, send a note to calendar@example.com so staff can check conflicts. The fictional internal page is https://example.test/copper-yard/calendar. A message may tag @copper_staff or #machine-slot.

What happens if I need more time?

First, inspect the next slot's availability. If it is open, you can increase the duration once. If another member is waiting, you must stop work and leave the machine safe. Staff can assist with storage. A delay does not justify removing the next member's reservation.

How does the waiting list work?

Choose the selected machine and press `join_waitlist`. The system will tell you when a slot opens. You have thirty minutes to accept it. The waiting list uses the same order for every member, although staff may alter access during a safety repair. Such a repair is a different event from a late cancellation.

What should I write in a fault report?

Write the observed symptom, the machine state, and the time. A clear report might say that the power button flashed twice and the fan stayed off. Avoid a broad answer such as “broken.” Staff need a specific result they can verify. A quick photo can assist staff if it contains no private material.

Who can delete a booking?

The member can delete an upcoming booking. Staff can remove a booking when the machine is unsafe or the workshop shuts down. The log will retain the time and reason, but it will not keep the project label after thirty days. This method gives staff sufficient history to inspect repeated conflicts.

How will Copper Yard judge the calendar?

The fictional workshop will run an eight-week trial. It will compare idle time, late starts, and abandoned slots with the previous paper sheet. It will also ask a basic question: did members find it easy to reserve the machine they needed? A strong outcome would be fewer collisions while added staff effort stays modest.

During the trial, staff will publish a weekly schedule snapshot so members can report gaps before arriving at the workshop. The archive will omit member names and project labels.

What does the calendar not solve?

It cannot prepare a beginner to operate a machine. Training, inspection, and supervision remain separate. It also cannot detect every unsafe setup. The calendar is one element of workshop operations. Its primary job is to display time fairly and support planning.
