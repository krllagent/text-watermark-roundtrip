# Interview Synthesis: Riders Wanted Certainty More Than More Scooters

*Fictional research synthesis: Wrenwheel, all participants, and all findings below are invented.*

We interviewed 15 fictional members of Wrenwheel, a small office scooter pool, after weekday bookings fell for two consecutive months. The original question was whether to buy four more scooters. The interviews suggest a different issue: people could see availability when booking, but they could not tell whether a scooter would actually be charged at pickup.

Nine participants described a recent low-battery surprise. Six of those nine had stopped booking for morning meetings. The relation is persuasive, but it is not clean proof of cause. Heavy morning riders may both drain batteries and encounter empty ones more often. The interviews also came from volunteers, who could have stronger opinions than the usual member.

> "I do not need more choices; I need one honest choice."

A concrete example came from Nia, a fictional analyst. She selected scooter 04 for an 08:30 trip because the app displayed it as available. At the rack, its battery showed 22%. She took a taxi for $18.40 and did not try the pool again that week. The booking record was technically correct: nobody else held the scooter. It was not sufficient for her actual aim.

We should begin with a two-week status test, not a purchase. A charger sensor will write `ready_after=80` when battery level exceeds 80%, and the app will display “ready,” “charging,” or “unknown.” An unknown scooter cannot be reserved for a trip starting within 30 minutes. This change is likely to lower apparent supply, even if it improves trust.

The trial costs a fictional $960, compared with $7,600 for four scooters. We will check completed bookings, pickup substitutions, and short post-trip responses. The primary success threshold is a 25% reduction in failed pickups without a fall of more than 10% in total trips.

One caveat remains: certainty could move demand into already busy periods. If morning completions improve while afternoon availability becomes worse, the team will need a different method. A single average would hide that outcome.
