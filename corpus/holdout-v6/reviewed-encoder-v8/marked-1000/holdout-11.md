# Experiment Diary: The Referral Prompt Worked Only After the Work Was Done

*Fictional experiment diary: Pebble Ledger, every participant, and all metrics below are invented.*

Pebble Ledger helps fictional market traders reconcile a day of cash sales. We wanted to increase referrals, so we tested the same prompt at two different moments. Group A saw it immediately after creating an account. Group B saw it after finishing a first daily total.

The trial included 240 new accounts split evenly by token `REF-A` or `REF-B`. Group A produced 7 referral links and 2 accepted invitations. Group B produced 29 links and 14 accepted invitations. The accepted rate per account was 1.7% versus 11.7%. Both groups received identical wording and a fictional £5 account credit.

> "Ask for trust after delivering evidence of value."

The basic explanation is causal and plausible: completing a total gave users a reason to recommend the product. But the relation is nuanced. Only 82 people in group B reached the prompt, so those viewers were already more engaged than the full group A audience. Timing and audience selection changed together. We cannot say the later moment alone created a sevenfold improvement.

One example came from a fictional flower seller named Orla. She ignored the opening prompt because she had not yet checked whether her totals matched. After closing a £684 day with a 60-pence discrepancy, she shared the tool with her business partner. The small error, not a polished welcome screen, was the useful proof for her.

We will keep the later prompt for now, but we will not add larger rewards. The next attempt will show the referral request to all users on day three, with a clear distinction between those who have and have not completed a total. That method should help identify whether task completion predicts sharing beyond simple persistence.

Our primary goal is 20 accepted invitations from 300 accounts without more than a 5% increase in support questions. A higher link count alone is not sufficient, because users can copy a link without sending it.
