# Usability Log: The Fictional Kiosk Was Easier After We Removed “Easy Mode”

*Fictional usability log: Blue Heron Gallery, its visitors, and all test data are invented.*

We tested a ticket kiosk with 18 fictional gallery visitors. The first screen offered “standard” and “easy mode.” Easy mode used larger text and fewer choices, but only 2 participants selected it. Four people who later struggled said the label felt like a judgment about their ability.

In the second prototype, we removed the modes. Every visitor saw the same basic purchase flow, with a persistent `TEXT_SIZE` control and a “show fewer ticket types” option. Fifteen of 18 completed a purchase without help, compared with 10 of 18 in the first round. Median time fell from 3 minutes 12 seconds to 2 minutes 21 seconds, a 26.6% reduction.

> "An accessible choice should describe the interface, not the person."

One example came from fictional visitor Bo, who increased text size before reading ticket descriptions. Bo did not need a shorter flow and kept all ticket types visible. The old design would have forced a choice between readable text and complete information.

The improvement is encouraging but not a direct measure of the label change. The second prototype also moved the card reader diagram beside the slot and used a brighter confirmation screen. Participants in round two were different people. We can say the combined design worked better in this small test; we cannot identify which alteration caused most of the result.

There were still three assisted purchases. Two visitors did not notice that concession tickets required a selection before payment. One inserted a card before choosing any ticket. We should add a numbered progress line, not another mode. The primary question is whether sequence becomes clear while visitors retain control over text and detail.

The next round will include 24 participants and a fictional research budget of $2,400. Continue if at least 85% finish without staff action and no participant loses a selected accessibility setting after going back. Speed alone is not sufficient.

The fictional log is at https://blueheron.example/research/kiosk. Comments would go to access@blueheron.example; @BlueHeronUX will publish the 85% check. No real visitor data are represented here.
