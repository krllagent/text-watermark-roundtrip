# A Fictional Founder’s Note After the Higher Price Failed

*Fictional founder letter: Vale Thread, its customers, and every financial result were invented for this corpus.*

Team,

We raised the monthly price of Vale Thread from $24 to $36 because eight interviewees told us the planning tool was “easily worth forty.” The new page ran for 28 days. It attracted 604 qualified visits, 21 trials, and 3 paid accounts. The previous page had converted 31 of 590 visits into trials and 9 into paid accounts.

> "Praise is not a purchase, and a test is not a verdict."

The 42% decline in trial starts is large enough that we should restore $24 today using flag `PRICE_BASELINE`. We should not conclude that no customer will pay more. The sample covers one channel, one month, and a page that changed both price and packaging. At $36 we also removed the setup call, believing experienced buyers preferred self-service. That alteration may have caused part of the drop.

One fictional prospect, Dev, gives us a useful example. He had budget approval for $40, but he left when the comparison table described the plan as “independent.” In his follow-up, independent sounded like “support is unavailable,” while we intended “no annual contract.” The price and the wording pointed in the same direction, so we cannot separate their effects.

Our next method is intentionally smaller. We will keep the $24 public plan and offer ten new trial users a $36 option that includes a 30-minute setup session. The primary query is whether at least 4 choose it after seeing both options. We will record the choice and reason, not infer willingness from compliments.

This result is disappointing, but it is also accurate enough to guide one decision. We spent a fictional $1,180 on traffic and design, which is less than the cost of building the advanced reporting requested in those interviews. The experiment helped us avoid a bigger assumption.

Please direct internal questions to pricing@valethread.example. The fictional log is at https://valethread.example/tests/price-03, and @ValeThreadTeam owns the next check. We will publish the 4-of-10 outcome even if it contradicts this explanation.
