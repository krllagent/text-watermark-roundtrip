# Case Study: A Smaller Fictional Menu Reduced Waste and Hid a Capacity Limit

*Fictional case study: Fern & Flour, its bakery, and all measurements were created for this corpus.*

Fern & Flour offered 18 lunch items every weekday. At close, staff discarded a fictional average of 46 portions, so the bakery tested a smaller 11-item menu for four weeks. Food waste fell to 27 portions per day, a 41.3% reduction, while revenue rose from $8,420 to $8,690 per week.

The obvious story is that fewer choices made demand easier to predict. That is partly supported by the records: ingredient substitutions fell from 32 to 9 per week, and preparation began 38 minutes later because the morning list was simpler.

> "A cleaner average can conceal the hour when the shelf went empty."

The caveat appeared on Thursdays. The smaller menu sold out of hot food by 13:10 on three of four Thursdays. For example, 17 fictional customers arrived after a nearby rehearsal and found only cold rolls. Nine purchased something; eight left. Their lost demand is not visible in waste or revenue, so the positive result may describe constrained capacity rather than better matching.

Code token `MENU-11` also changed which items were visible at the entrance. Staff moved the chalkboard closer to the street because eleven names fit on one panel. That plain display could have increased revenue independently of menu size. The experiment changed assortment, preparation, and presentation together.

We will keep eleven core items but add a Thursday reserve of 20 hot portions. The reserve begins at 12:45 and should not be displayed before the main batch reaches five portions. This simple method gives us a clear next question without a large menu change. We will check waste, stockout minutes, walkaways, and revenue in 30-minute periods.

Success for the next six weeks is fewer than 32 discarded portions per day and no more than 5% of observed visitors leaving during a hot-food stockout. The primary goal is to improve availability without hiding waste. The observer will record only counts, not faces or descriptions. A strong revenue week is insufficient if late customers repeatedly have no reasonable choice.

The fictional case is at https://fernflour.example/cases/menu-11. Questions would go to kitchen@fernflour.example, and #FernFlourTest records Thursday outcomes. The reserve adds at most $96 in weekly ingredient cost.
