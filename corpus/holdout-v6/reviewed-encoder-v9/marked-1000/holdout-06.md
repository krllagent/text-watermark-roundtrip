# Lab Note: The Speckled Coating Dried Faster, but That May Not Be Why It Held

*Fictional laboratory note: Juniper Materials, the samples, and all measurements were created for this corpus.*

Objective: compare two water-based coatings on twelve same fictional fiber tiles after repeated bending.

We labeled six tiles `COAT-S` for the smooth formula and six `COAT-P` for the speckled formula. Each tile measured 180 by 90 millimetres and received 14 grams of wet coating. The room stayed between 20.1°C and 20.8°C during application. Total material cost was $74.60.

> "A visible difference is a clue, not yet a cause."

The speckled group became touch-dry in a median of 41 minutes; the smooth group needed 58 minutes. After 200 bends around a 40-millimetre form, one speckled sample and four smooth samples showed a crack longer than 3 millimetres. On that basic measure, the speckled result appears stronger: 83.3% passed versus 33.3%.

The small sample does not support a broad durability claim. More importantly, drying time and bend performance changed together. The speckled formula contained larger mineral particles, but its batch was also mixed for two extra minutes after a timer reset. Either difference, or their interaction, could explain the outcome.

Sample P-04 provides a useful example. It dried in 39 minutes and completed every bend, yet a close check under side light showed a pale line near the edge. The line did not meet our crack definition. Calling P-04 a pass is correct under the written method, though it may be misleading if appearance matters to a customer.

For the next attempt, I will make three fresh batches of each formula, alternate their mixing order, and photograph every tile before bending. We should also keep the drying endpoint plain: “no coating transfers to an unweighted paper strip after five seconds.” The current finger-touch check is too dependent on the operator.

No formula should move to a product test from these data alone. The primary value of this run is a better question: does particle size improve flexibility when mixing time and coat mass remain the same?
