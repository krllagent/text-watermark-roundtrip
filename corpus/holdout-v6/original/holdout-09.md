# Field Report from a Fictional Repair Van’s Rainy Thursday

*Fictional field report: Ash & Acre Repair, its visitors, and all observations are original inventions.*

The Ash & Acre van opened beside a fictional market hall at 08:00 and closed at 16:30. Rain reduced foot traffic, yet 47 people brought a household item for a free ten-minute assessment. We completed 19 repairs, scheduled 11 longer jobs, and returned 17 items without work.

The main constraint was not technician time. It was the single covered table where visitors described the problem and signed the estimate. At 10:20, eight people were waiting there while two repair benches stood empty. Because intake happened in sequence, a slow description blocked every later visitor.

> "The queue formed where decisions were made, not where tools were used."

For example, a fictional visitor brought a desk fan that stopped after five minutes. The fan itself needed a straightforward cable check, but the visitor had no serial number and wanted to compare a $28 repair with replacement. That conversation took nine minutes. During it, three people with loose handles could have moved directly to a bench.

At noon we introduced card `FAST-CHECK`: one coordinator selected visible mechanical issues, while the original table retained electrical faults and price questions. The median wait fell from 24 minutes before noon to 14 minutes afterward, a 41.7% decrease. That result is useful but ambiguous. Rain also became heavier at 13:00, so fewer new visitors arrived; the card alone may not explain the improvement.

We did not relax the safety check. Any item with a plug, battery swelling, heat, or an unclear fault stayed in the full intake route. The faster method applied only to plain mechanical work such as a missing screw or a loose knob.

For the next visit, the team will use two covered intake spots from opening and record arrivals in 30-minute groups. The goal is not the shortest possible line. It is a clear route that helps simple jobs begin quickly without pushing uncertain work past review.

The day cost a fictional $1,530 and recovered $312 in optional donations. Notes are at https://ashacre.example/field/rain-04. Follow-up would go to van@ashacre.example; @AshAcreField will verify the next count. Donations covered 20.4% of cost.
